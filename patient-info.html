Appointments

How to book

Appointments can be booked online any time using the Book Appointment button, or by phoning the practice during opening hours.

Whether longer appointments must be requested, and how — to be supplied

Telehealth

Telehealth appointments are booked the same way as a face-to-face appointment, and the Telehealth button takes you straight into the consult.

Eligibility rules — e.g. existing patients seen in the last 12 months — to be confirmed

What to bring

Medicare card, any concession or DVA card, private health details if relevant, a list of your current medicines, and any letters, results or paperwork you have been given.

Medicare details

Please make sure the practice has your current Medicare number, expiry and card position, and tell reception if your card or address changes.

Cancellations and no-shows

Please give as much notice as you can if you cannot attend, so the appointment can be offered to someone else.

Required notice period and any non-attendance fee — to be confirmed

Requests & paperwork

Repeat prescriptions

Repeat prescriptions can be requested online. Requests are not automatically approved — every request is reviewed by a clinician and may require an appointment.

Turnaround time and how the script is issued — to be confirmed

Which medicines always require an appointment — to be supplied

Repeat referrals

Repeat referrals to a specialist you already see can be requested online. A referral for a new problem, or to a specialist you have not seen before, needs an appointment.

Turnaround time and how the referral is provided — to be confirmed

Medical certificates

Medical certificates require an appointment. A certificate cannot be backdated or issued without an assessment.

Forms and paperwork

Insurance forms, Centrelink paperwork, workers compensation forms and similar documents usually need a longer appointment.

Paperwork turnaround and fees — to be supplied

Results & privacy

Results and recalls

Results are not given out over the phone unless the practice has told you otherwise. If your doctor needs to discuss a result, the practice will contact you to arrange an appointment.

How results and recalls are handled, and who to contact — to be supplied

Reminder/recall system and how to opt out — to be supplied

Privacy and confidentiality

Your health information is confidential and is handled in line with the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles.

Full privacy policy — how records are stored, who can access them, and how to request your records — to be drafted with you

Feedback and complaints

Feedback is welcome. If something has not gone well, please tell the practice first so it can be looked into.

How to give feedback — form, phone or in person — to be decided

If you are not satisfied with the practice’s response you may contact the Office of the Health Ombudsman (Queensland).
